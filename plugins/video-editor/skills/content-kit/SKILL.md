# Content kit

Give the person everything they need before they film: what to say, how to open, how to caption it and how to shoot it. Plain words, their voice, nothing invented.

**Done means:** one document with all five parts below, in their voice, every hook and script specific to their customer's problem, and no claim, number or result they didn't give you. Offer it as a file they can download and print.

## 1. Get the inputs

If they pasted research (from Muse or anywhere else: what's working in their niche, top creators, popular hooks, their customers' questions), read it and use it. Then ask only what's missing.

If they have nothing, ask these five, one message, numbered:
1. What do you sell, and to whom?
2. What's the problem your best customers come to you with, in the words they use?
3. What do people get wrong about it?
4. What's one result or story you're proud of (real, with whatever numbers you're happy to share)?
5. What do you want a viewer to do after watching (follow, book, visit, message you)?

## 2. Write the kit

### Who it's for
One sentence: the viewer, their problem and the outcome they want. Everything below serves that sentence.

### Hook bank (20 hooks)
Four of each kind:
- **The problem:** names the pain the viewer feels ("Your fig isn't dying from the light").
- **The mistake or myth:** what people get wrong ("Stop watering on a schedule").
- **The outcome:** what they want, made concrete ("A fig that grows new leaves in a month").
- **The story or proof:** only from what they told you ("I've killed 40 plants so you don't have to").
- **The contrarian take:** the twist they believe and others don't.

For each, give the spoken first line (12 words or fewer) and a text hook for the screen (7 words or fewer, never a copy of the spoken line). Mark the five strongest with ★.

### Five scripts
Each 30-45 seconds (80-110 words), built to the Hook Stack:
- the hook line;
- one line of context, so the topic is obvious;
- the twist ("it's not X, it's Y");
- three short points, one idea per line;
- the payoff;
- one simple closing line that asks for the action from question 5 (no hard sell).

Format them to read off a phone: one short sentence per line, a blank line between beats, no words they wouldn't say out loud. Put the text-hook option above each script.

### Caption style
- Three example post captions (1-3 short lines each) in their voice.
- 3-5 hashtag ideas per script: specific to the niche and the customer, never generic tags like #viral.
- A short voice guide: words they use, words to avoid, whether they use emoji.

### Shot list for filming day
- **Where:** a quiet room, light in front of them (a window facing them), a simple background with some depth.
- **Phone:** upright (vertical), back camera at eye level on a stand, 1080p at 30 fps with HDR Video off (iPhone: Settings → Camera → Record Video), lens wiped, about an arm and a half away so head and shoulders fill the frame.
- **Sound:** close the windows, turn off fans; a clip-on mic if they have one.
- **Each take:** look at the lens, wait two seconds, then speak. If they stumble, pause, say "let me start over", and go again from the start of the sentence: the editor cuts all of that.
- **Order:** film every script twice, hook lines three times with different energy.
- **Checklist:** charged phone, enough storage, Do Not Disturb on, water, script open on a second device or printed.

## 3. Hand it over

Share the kit as a file. Then say: "When you've filmed one, send me the video and I'll edit it." (The video-editor skill does that part.)

## Rules

- Never invent results, client names, numbers, credentials or quotes. If a hook needs proof they haven't given, write it with a gap ("I've helped [number] clients…") and flag it.
- Keep claims about their field accurate and modest; when something needs checking, say so.
- Research numbers (views, skip rates) from creators are those creators' claims; don't repeat them as facts in scripts.
